## Q1 Deflated and probabilistic Sharpe ratio

TAKEN OVER (PSR paper, Eq. (11), printed p. 9; readable Appendix A.3, p. 20): with per-period excess-return Sharpe s, reference s0, T observations, return skewness g3 and ordinary kurtosis g4 (normal = 3):

`PSR(s0) = Phi((s - s0) * sqrt(T - 1) / sqrt(1 - g3*s + ((g4 - 1)/4)*s^2))`.

TAKEN OVER (DSR paper, Eq. (1), p. 7; Eq. (2), p. 8; implementation snippet p. 13): let V be variance across the trials' estimated Sharpes, N the number of independent trials, gamma Euler's constant, and Phi_inv the standard Normal quantile:

`E[max Sharpe] ~= mean_trial_Sharpe + sqrt(V) * ((1-gamma)*Phi_inv(1-1/N) + gamma*Phi_inv(1-1/(N*e)))`.

`DSR = PSR(s0)` with `s0 = sqrt(V) * ((1-gamma)*Phi_inv(1-1/N) + gamma*Phi_inv(1-1/(N*e)))` under the zero-mean null. This is an expected-maximum approximation, not an exact finite-N quantile. The paper distinguishes dispersion across trial estimates from the selected strategy's return moments.

TAKEN OVER (PSR p. 9 and vectorbt metrics documentation): Sharpe inputs are at the return sampling frequency, not annualized; kurtosis is not excess kurtosis. REASONING: if an annualization convention used sqrt(A), undo it for s and s0, and divide annualized trial-Sharpe variance by A; this conversion does not validate annualization under serial dependence. A count of trades is not the T of a daily-return series. Overlap accounting belongs upstream of the statistic.

TESTED (static comparison using `cat`/`nl`/`sed`, two library files; zero numerical cases): [vectorbt `returns/metrics.py`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/master/vectorbt/returns/metrics.py), lines 12–16 and 19–38, has the expected-maximum expression and the PSR denominator above. It explicitly documents per-period inputs and ordinary kurtosis. [pypbo `pypbo/pbo.py`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/esvhd/pypbo/master/pypbo/pbo.py), lines 451–508, has the PSR expression and sets its DSR threshold from `sharpe_std * expected_max(N)`. Its convenience function lines 511–539 uses all matrix columns for N and sample standard deviation across their Sharpes, and returns column-wise moments. REASONING: it is not an effective-N estimator and should not be treated as a scalar-selected-strategy oracle without checking argument shapes. The inspected algebra matches the displayed expressions at this scope; estimator conventions, tails, invalid cases, release parity and numerical correctness remain not verified.

TESTED (discovery only): queries for the exact PSR title plus "erratum", and DSR plus "errata", "correction" and "corrigendum", did not yield a read official correction. REASONING: that is not proof that no erratum exists. No published erratum was verified in this run. The PSR PDF's equation typography extracted poorly; Appendix A.3 provides readable corroborating code, not executed here.

REASONING: unresolved contract cases include T too small, zero return variance, non-finite moments, non-positive denominator, zero trial dispersion, N=1 (the approximation has a quantile endpoint), fractional effective N, missing trials and correlated daily returns. Do not silently clamp these into a passing probability. The paper's asymptotic calculation is not evidence that the complete F1 selection procedure has a 5% false-pass rate.

## Q2 Effective number of trials

TAKEN OVER (local spec): raw N spans the full registry across markets; exact technical repeats are separate from new trials. Effective N is to come from clustering correlations of trial daily returns. The ONC paper's SSRN retrieval failed twice in the earlier run; the amendment allows N and continuation. Its precise recursion, cluster-number criterion, random restarts, seed handling and source-stated time bound remain **not verified**. A k-means package is not, by itself, an ONC implementation or a justified effective-N conversion.

TAKEN OVER ([poolr `meff` official documentation](https://ozancinar.github.io/poolr/reference/meff.html), unnumbered web equations, no paper page): it accepts a symmetric test-dependence matrix R or supplied eigenvalues. Alternatives include Nyholt, Li–Ji, Gao and Galwey. Li–Ji's paper remains N; these are claims about poolr's documented methods, not independent verification against each original paper. With k tests and eigenvalues lambda:

- `Nyholt: m = 1 + (k-1)*(1 - sample_variance(lambda)/k)`.
- `Li-Ji: m = sum f(abs(lambda_i)); f(x) = I(x>=1) + x-floor(x)`.
- `Gao: smallest m whose leading-eigenvalue sum / total sum > C`, documented default C=0.995.
- `Galwey: m = (sum sqrt(max(lambda_i,0)))^2 / sum max(lambda_i,0)`.

TAKEN OVER (same documentation): non-integer estimates are floored; dependence among test statistics or p-values is not generically the same as correlation of the raw inputs. REASONING: these deterministic eigenvalue transformations are sensitivity/reference candidates, not authorized replacements for the clustering-based contract. All-identical, independent, negative-correlated, rank-deficient and missing-overlap trial sets can distinguish the methods; no such numerical fixture was run here.

REASONING (explicit dense-algorithm bounds, not source benchmarks): for T synchronized days and N trials, storing returns costs O(TN), correlations O(N^2), straightforward correlation construction O(TN^2), and dense symmetric eigen-decomposition O(N^3) time/O(N^2) working memory. The formula after decomposition is O(N), plus sorting O(N log N) where required. These bounds apply to all four eigenvalue alternatives; they are not an ONC bound.

TESTED (static read of `mljs/kmeans` initialization and `mljs/hclust/src/agnes.ts`, three files, zero runtime cases): k-means uses random initialization including seeded k-means++; AGNES builds/replaces dense distance matrices and repeatedly selects a smallest distance, with no RNG in the inspected loop. REASONING: for R restarts, I iterations, K clusters and N-dimensional correlation-row features, ordinary k-means work is O(R*I*K*N^2), before a cluster-number search or recursive refinement. Silhouette/distance matrices can add O(N^2) space. This AGNES structure has O(N^3) dense work and O(N^2) peak matrix space. Fixed input order matters for ties. Seeded repeatability across versions/platforms and an end-to-end ONC cost remain not verified.

REASONING: I90 makes matrix size a real design constraint. One dense float64 correlation matrix alone needs `8*N*N` bytes, before copies, JS arrays, daily returns or decomposer workspace. Correlation-based effective N also needs an explicit common-calendar/missing-history policy; substituting pairwise correlations with incompatible histories can produce a non-PSD matrix. None of these choices can be settled from a package name.

## Q4 Overfitting and data-snooping tests

PBO is the fraction/probability of a non-positive rank logit. REASONING: a winner-only curve cannot supply the other trials' ranks; the combinatorial number of splits grows as `choose(S,S/2)`. ## Q5 Null campaigns and planted edges

TAKEN OVER (Aparicio–López de Prado 2018, section 3, pp. 55–56): their simulation uses multiple return processes with noise/jumps and introduces one superior strategy through higher expected returns, then evaluates model selection across repeated simulations. It is a useful primary example of known-ground-truth power assessment. REASONING: its loss/model-selection experiment does not certify an entry/exit search with F1 costs, sizing, walk-forward, effective N and kill layers.

TAKEN OVER (official implementation sources):

- [SciPy `permutation_test`](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.permutation_test.html) exposes independent-sample, paired-assignment and pairing permutations, custom statistic, resample count and seeded generator. Its null depends on exchangeability; it is a primitive, not a ready-made trading campaign.
- [vectorbt `signals/generators.py`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polakowo/vectorbt/master/vectorbt/signals/generators.py), RAND/RANDX/RANDNX, supplies seeded random entries/exits. This is primary implementation evidence for a random-entry baseline, not an academic claim that its output has zero expected net profit.
- [arch `bootstrap/base.py`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bashtage/arch/main/arch/bootstrap/base.py), StationaryBootstrap lines 2213–2330, names Politis–Romano and uses mean block length with restart probability `1/block_size`; its internal comment identifies geometric block lengths. MovingBlockBootstrap uses fixed blocks without wrapping; circular blocks wrap. The original 1994 scanned paper remains N, so detailed method claims here are taken from arch rather than attributed to a paper that was not read.
- The DSR paper's p. 13 snippet repeatedly samples Normal trial Sharpes and compares simulated maxima with its analytical approximation. That tests a component approximation, not a complete strategy search.

REASONING: unrestricted shuffling of returns destroys serial dependence and may destroy cross-market dependence if done column by column. Random-entry baselines should hold exit/cost/sizing rules fixed; random timings can still load on drift, funding, volatility or holding-period effects. Block bootstrap approximately retains local serial dependence under its assumptions; it does not preserve every autocorrelation exactly or repair regime shifts. A null needs an explicit zero-edge construction/recentering, joint-column resampling and a block-length rule. Reusing the observed winning return series does not reproduce the original search's multiplicity.

REASONING: the complete-campaign experimental unit is one frozen search through all choices and gates; false-pass frequency counts campaigns with any surviving null candidate. Power counts detection of the planted causal/predictive edge, not just a positive mean pasted onto a completed equity curve. Repeat across seed sets, dependence regimes, effect sizes and costs; report denominators and uncertainty, not only whether one seed found an edge. This is a research-design gap assessment, not a proposed implementation schedule. No null campaign or planted-edge experiment was run.
