### 3.2 NEW readiness (F1, F2, F3)

  - `_start_handover` (912-917) fills `need` with the endpoint's cadence streams.
  - `_handover_reader` removes each stream on its first message (982). `t_ready` is set once `need` is empty (983-984).
- `_handover_state` (991-1016) replaces `_switch_reason`:
  - **Not ready:** no switch by any path. Abort with stage `buffer_full` if the buffer is full (1000). Abort with stage `not_ready` once `HANDOVER_READY_MAX_S` has passed since NEW opened (1001-1002). Otherwise wait until that deadline.
  - **Ready:** as before, `caught_up`, then `max_time`, then `buffer_full`. But `HANDOVER_MAX_S` now counts from `t_ready`, not from NEW's first message (1011). - OLD's end during a handover switches (`old_ended`) only to a ready NEW (1044-1046).
- Why: the review found F1 (a silent NEW is never given up and is taken over when OLD ends), F2 (a NEW without some streams is switched to) and F3 (the switch comes before a late stream arrived). Rule 2 of the spec covers F1 by this readiness rule.

### 3.3 Aborting a NEW (F1, F2)

`_abort_handover` (1147-1182):
- Cancels NEW's reader. `recv()` and the connect are cancel-safe. A cancelled connect aborts its transport (taken over: websockets 17.2 `asyncio/client.py`, the `except asyncio.CancelledError: connection.transport.abort()` in `connect`).
- Closes NEW in a background task. The data path never waits for a close handshake.
- Sets `retry_at = now + HANDOVER_RETRY_S` (1155).
- Logs one line that names the missing streams, and appends a record to `handover_failures` with the stage, the missing streams, the buffered count and OLD's age.
- Awaits the reader only at the end, so a cancel cannot skip the close.

### 3.4 OLD ends while no ready NEW exists (F1/A12, F5)

- **During a handover** (`_during_handover`, 1047-1070): NEW is dropped as in 3.3 (stage `old_ended`), with one line. Then today's reconnect path runs at once:
  - A close frame returns from `_read`, and `run_endpoint` connects again without a sleep.
  - An error is re-raised into `run_endpoint`'s error path (its line, backoff 1 s, `book.restart()` for public, aggTrade gap to REST).
- **No handover pending** (for example between failed handovers, or Binance's 24 h cut): `_read` now logs a clean close with OLD's age (898-902), then connects again at once. The error path keeps its existing line.

- **Per-connection arrival times:**

  - `_switch` hands NEW's times to the endpoint (1084).
- **`_guard`** (923-953), one task per endpoint connection, started in `run_endpoint` (832). It stays across switches:

Reasoning: the abort path is the same as a TCP drop, which A15 and f08 test from the server side. The watchdog firing *during* a handover is not tested directly.
  - NEW is not watched before the switch; readiness covers it.

### 3.7 Stop within 5 s (rule 5, the review's O3)

- `_guard` on stop (946-953):
  - Notifies a pending handover, which then sees stop at once.
  - Gives `_read` `STOP_GRACE_S` to return by itself on the next message.
  - If it does not, `_read` is waiting in `recv()` on a silent connection: the guard starts a background close and aborts the transport after `CLOSE_WAIT_S`.
- `_connect` (855-880) and `_sleep_unless_stop` (882-886): a connect in progress and the error backoff (up to 60 s) end when stop is set.
- `run_endpoint`'s except clause breaks without a line when stop is set (845-846).
- Every connection end closes in the background (840), and `_drain_closing` (1219-1235) bounds those closes at the end of `run_endpoint`: `CLOSE_WAIT_S`, then abort. The old code waited for every background close without a bound (close_timeout 10 s each).
- **Robust to a second cancel.** `asyncio.run`'s teardown cancels `run_endpoint` a second time after `main()` has already cancelled it. Three measures cover this:
  - The per-connection cleanup starts every close before its first `await` (836-843).
  - `_end_endpoint` no longer awaits (1184-1199).
  - A background-close task that is cancelled, even before it ran, aborts its connection through a done-callback (1210-1213). `_drain_closing` also aborts on cancel (1226-1228).
- Why: tested, with the review's A50 as the check. A50 failed during development (a connection left open after SIGTERM plus teardown) until these were in place.
- Files are flushed by `main()` as before (`rec.close()`).

### 3.8 Changes made for the existing tests, not asked for by the spec (tested)

- **Fewer tasks per handover.**
  - Before: a task waiting on an `asyncio.Event`, re-created on every NEW message, plus a stop task.
  - Now: `_during_handover` waits on a plain future that the reader resolves (`Handover.notify`, 576-580), and the watchdog notifies it at stop.
  - Why: A22 bounds the tasks alive at a switch at baseline + 12. Readiness makes the two endpoints' handovers overlap more (market waits for `markPrice`), and the peak reached 27 against a bound of 26. Now 25 (section 5 row 6).
- **`_start_handover` is its own method.** A local variable in the long-running `_read` frame kept the last Handover alive (A22 found 2 alive instead of 0).
- **A wall-clock step during the handshake does not move a connection's open time.** `_connect` (855-880) returns the open's wall time as the attempt's wall time plus the handshake's monotonic duration. Without a clock step this equals the open time, as at 984ccde. Why: A20 steps the clock while the client may still be in the handshake (6.3).
- **`Endpoint` is no longer an async context manager.** `run_endpoint` closes the current connection explicitly (840).

## 6. Conflicts and problems

### 6.1 Conflicts inside the spec: A11, A12 and A13 cannot pass under the spec's rules and defaults

The acceptance list says A11, A12 and A13 now pass. Rules 1 and 2 and the default `HANDOVER_READY_MAX_S` = 30 s rule that out; the tests were written before the constant existed. I followed the rules and left the tests unchanged, as the spec says to. Why each test is wrong:

- **A11** (`test_a11_new_opens_but_sends_nothing`) waits at most 8 s after the silent NEW opened: `HANDOVER_MAX_S + HANDOVER_CONNECT_TIMEOUT_S + HANDOVER_RETRY_S + 3.0` = 2 + 2 + 1 + 3. In that time it expects one recorded failure and a retry. Rule 1 aborts a NEW that is not ready only 30 s after it opened, and the test cannot lower that constant.
  - Tested, failure on the final code: `(0, False, False) != (1, True, True)`. With 2 s (row 11): pass.
- **A13** (`test_a13_new_sends_only_some_streams`) waits 8 s per endpoint for a successful switch (`handover_done`). Rule 1 forbids switching to the NEW without `markPrice`/`bookTicker`, and aborts it at 30 s; the retry would switch later.
  - Tested: `timed out after 8 s waiting for: market handover`. With 2 s: pass.
- **A12** (`test_a12_silent_new_then_old_closed_by_server`) first asserts `reasons == ["old_ended"]` ("the silent NEW was taken over"), that is, a switch to the silent NEW. Rules 1, 2 and 4 forbid exactly that switch.
  - With the fix, the silent NEW is dropped and the endpoint reconnects at once. The test's `RECONNECT_AFTER_S` (1.2 s) then starts a normal handover on the new connection, so the reasons are `['caught_up']`.
  - Its second assertion, the substance of F1 (more than 100 aggTrades in the 0.5-3 s after OLD's close), holds. Tested in row 11 with only the first assertion recorded instead of raised. f07 covers the same attack with assertions that fit the rules.

Doing otherwise would have meant one of:
- a production default below the spec's 30 s, chosen to fit an 8 s test window;
- tying the readiness limit to the test settings;
- recording a non-switch as a switch.

All three would make the tests pass without the behaviour the spec asks for, so I did none of them.

### 6.3 Two timing-sensitive tests: A20 and A22 (tested)

- **A20 has a race that predates this work.**
  - A20 moves the collector's wall clock as soon as the fake server has registered the connection. The server registers it right after sending the handshake response, before the client has processed that response.
  - If the test's 5 ms poll lands in that window, the clock moves before the client takes the connection's open time. OLD then counts as fresh, no handover starts, and the test times out at "handovers failing".
  - Measured over 40 fresh-process runs (row 7): 1 at 984ccde, 4 at b9a6241. That difference is not significant: Fisher's exact test, two-sided, p = 0.36 (computed).
- **First fix (6aefe1a): count from the connect attempt.** This removed the A20 race (40/40), but it moved every handover earlier by the handshake's duration.
  - A22 sets `RECONNECT_AFTER_S` = 0.25 s before `start()` and takes its baseline task count right after the book syncs. The sync comes about 0.5 s after the first depth event (`load_snapshot` sleeps 0.5 s). So the second handover starts within a few ms of the baseline snapshot.
  - Moved a few ms earlier, the handovers were in flight at the snapshot in 5 of 10 runs, and A22 failed: "not back at baseline" (row 6a). One full run at 6aefe1a failed A22 (row 4a).
- **Final fix (41273a4).** The open's wall time is the attempt's wall time plus the handshake's monotonic duration.
  - Normal timing is as at b9a6241 and 984ccde (open time). In the in-process series A22's snapshot is clean (10/10, row 6a); in fresh processes it can still catch a handover, as at b9a6241 (row 6b, below).
  - A wall-clock step during the handshake no longer moves the start of `RECONNECT_AFTER_S`, so A20 does not race (40/40).
  - In production this matters only if the system clock steps during a handshake (reasoning).
- Both tests stay as they are. My own f14 also waits for the first message before moving the clock.
- **A22 still fails sometimes when it is the first test of a fresh process, on every version measured** (row 6b). Three modes:
  1. **Baseline snapshot.** The baseline is taken while handovers already run (`RECONNECT_AFTER_S` 0.25 s) and can catch one in flight. Reasoning: readiness makes a market handover last until NEW's first `markPrice` (up to 100 ms in the fake), so this should be likelier than at 984ccde. The counts (5 of 28 against 1 of 12) do not show a significant difference.
  2. **Task-peak bound.** Seen once at 984ccde.
  3. **TypeError, seen once on 41273a4.** The traceback was not captured and 30 further runs did not repeat it. Reasoning from the code: `OrderBook.load_snapshot` sets `synced` with `prev_u = None` when every buffered event is older than the snapshot. That happens when the mocked REST snapshot carries the newest depth id before that tick's event arrived. `AdvBase.start()` records `book0` at that moment, and `assert_book_matches_server` then runs `server.updates(None, ...)` with `None < id`. This is in the test harness and the book code, not in this change, and could occur at 984ccde too (NOT VERIFIED there).
  - I left A22 as it is: every remedy would mean editing the test or changing the readiness the spec requires.

## 7. Constants and defaults: guesses to calibrate

None of these values is verified against real Binance (NOT VERIFIED). They should be calibrated against the largest silence per stream measured on the VPS for each of the four symbols. That measurement was not done here, and no command for it is given because it could not be tested on a VPS sample (F01).

| constant | default | used for | basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HANDOVER_READY_MAX_S` | 30 s | NEW must have delivered every cadence stream this long after opening | spec default. Taken over (`docs/phase2-diagnostics-storage-2026-10-09.md`, one BTCUSDT hour, average rates, not maximum gaps): `markPrice@1s` 3,600 lines, `depth@100ms` 35,276, `aggTrade` 29,501, `bookTicker` 946,436, `forceOrder` 14. aggTrade is part of readiness, so a symbol whose trades pause for 30 s would fail handovers; they are then retried (NOT VERIFIED for HYPE, SOL, ETH) |
| `SILENCE_MAX_S` | 60 s | a watched stream silent this long aborts the connection | spec default. 60 times `markPrice@1s`'s cadence. A false trigger costs today's reconnect: about 1 s backoff, an aggTrade REST fill, a book restart for public |
| `WATCHDOG_TICK_S` | 1.0 s | how often the watchdog looks (also at least 10 times per `SILENCE_MAX_S`) | my choice: detection at most about one tick after `SILENCE_MAX_S`. Tested scaled: abort within `SILENCE_MAX_S` + 0.3 s at a 1 s limit (f01, f02) |
| `STOP_GRACE_S` | 0.5 s | at stop, time for `_read` to return on its next message before a silent connection is closed | my choice |
| `CLOSE_WAIT_S` | 1.0 s | at stop, a close handshake gets this long, then the transport is aborted | my choice. Tested: a stop with the server ignoring the close ends within 5 s (f09, f10); without the abort it took 10.5 s (M6) |
| `HANDOVER_MAX_S` | 3 s (unchanged) | now counted from readiness | a forced switch can now come up to `HANDOVER_READY_MAX_S` + `HANDOVER_MAX_S` after NEW opened (reasoning) |

Stream names (taken over): the same document lists the real raw stream names `btcusdt@bookTicker`, `btcusdt@depth@100ms`, `btcusdt@aggTrade`, `btcusdt@markPrice@1s` and `btcusdt@forceOrder`. These are exactly the subscribed names that readiness and the watchdog compare against. If Binance ever sent other names, every handover would fail readiness and the watchdog would fire every `SILENCE_MAX_S` (reasoning).
