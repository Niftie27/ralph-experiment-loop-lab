# Slow Copy-Trading Distinction

Tomas clarified that when he is still interested in copy-trading, he does not mean fast Hyperliquid perp trader mirroring. He means following slow accumulators:

> following slow accumulators (mid-cap spot, holding weeks)

This distinction changes the verdict.

Fast perp copy-trading should remain heavily suspect because it reintroduces latency, leaderboard survivorship bias, hidden hedge risk, fees, crowding, and low learning value.

Slow accumulator following is a valid patient-retail branch:

- latency is less damaging because trades resolve over days/weeks;
- entry can be hours or a day late and still capture much of the move;
- risk can be paper-tested;
- it aligns with Tomas's goal of finding smart-money wallets rather than racing market microstructure.

But the branch has three core weaknesses:

1. Exit and distribution risk: the smart wallet may sell into later retail attention and Tomas can become exit liquidity.
2. Selection and label bias: "smart money" sets are often survivorship-biased, vendor-defined, or chosen because they already won.
3. Liquidity/data coverage risk: mid-cap spot has thinner data and liquidity than majors, while good opportunities may be fragmented across chains, CEXs, and private wallets.

RALPH implication:

Do not reject copy-trading as a single category. Split it into:

- fast copy-trading: reject or radar-only unless proven otherwise;
- slow accumulator following: valid candidate branch, but only through cohort construction, exit-shadowing, beta decomposition, and out-of-sample forward testing.
