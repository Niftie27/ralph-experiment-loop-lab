## Summary

This source is a wiki-ready strategy map for a patient retail crypto participant who will not compete on latency and can wait weeks for a trade to resolve.

Its central claim is that RALPH should choose strategy branches by edge speed, not by asset:

- fast-informational edges should be avoided;
- slow-informational edges may be shadowable;
- structural edges are the most robust for patient retail because they do not depend on seeing information first.

The source maps six strategy archetypes:

- funding-rate / basis harvesting;
- staking / real yield as baseline;
- token-unlock positioning;
- smart-money accumulation;
- copy-vaults / auto-copy;
- airdrop / points farming.

The most relevant RALPH implication is that wallet shadowing should not default to famous wallets, memecoins, BTC/ETH direction, or latency-sensitive event timing. The first serious branches are:

- structural funding/basis baseline;
- smart-money accumulation cohort;
- aggregate cohort flow rather than single-wallet copying;
- token-unlock positioning as a slow public-information branch.

## Critical Notes

The document is a strategy hypothesis map, not proof of edge.

RALPH should preserve these caveats:

- every wallet-based signal must pass out-of-sample forward testing;
- "smart money" labels are usually survivorship-biased vendor sets;
- slow accumulation shifts the main failure mode from late entry to bad exit/distribution;
- structural strategies do not fail through latency, but through yield compression, tail risk, depeg, liquidation, and operational cost.

## RALPH Use

Treat this as the canonical patient-retail scope document until superseded:

- no memecoin focus;
- no latency games;
- BTC/ETH only as benchmark/context unless a specific branch proves otherwise;
- prefer slower edges and structural edges;
- map all branches into the same activity-universe, screening, frozen-cohort, forward-test architecture.
