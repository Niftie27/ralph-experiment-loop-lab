# Patient-Retail Strategy Map

The patient-retail strategy map reframes RALPH around a user who:

- will not compete on milliseconds or seconds;
- can wait days or weeks for a trade to play out;
- wants smart, simple, low-speed strategies;
- prefers research, paper-trading, and no-key validation before capital.

## Core Ordering

For Tomas, the preference order is:

1. Structural edges.
2. Slow-informational edges.
3. Delegated strategies.
4. Fast-informational edges only as radar or discard.

## Why This Matters

Fast trading asks Tomas to beat professional latency and execution.

Patient-retail research asks a different question:

> Which edges remain intact when Tomas enters hours or days after detection?

This makes strategy selection more important than venue selection.

## First Serious Branches

### Structural Baseline

Funding-rate / basis harvesting should be treated as the no-prediction branch. It is not automatically safe, but it is useful as a structural baseline to compare against wallet-shadowing alpha.

### Smart-Money Cohort

Smart-money accumulation should be tested as cohort flow, not one-wallet copying. The strongest version tracks aggregate accumulation and stablecoin-to-alt rotation across a qualified cohort.

### Token Unlocks

Token unlocks are slow public information. The naive trade is crowded, but the branch is useful for learning how public schedules, liquidity, float, and recipient type interact.

### Delegated Copy

Copy-vaults and auto-copy platforms are useful as prior art and reference implementations. They should not be trusted as evidence of copyability without independent forward testing.

## Non-Goals

- memecoin sniping;
- Trump-branded token research;
- copy trading famous one-off wallets;
- leaderboard-first universe selection;
- live trading before paper-trade evidence.
